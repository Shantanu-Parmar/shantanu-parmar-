Current Focus

Three threads, one instrument bench

My work moves between observing the universe, modeling how spacecraft propulsion behaves, and teaching machines to see clearly in the dark.

§ 01

Astrophysics & Gravitational Waves

Pulsar timing, white dwarf binaries, protoplanetary disk chemistry, and transient noise classification in LIGO data.

§ 02

Electric Propulsion

Machine-learning performance prediction for low-power Hall effect thrusters and plasma plume simulation.

§ 03

Imaging & Computer Vision

Event-based star tracking, generative restoration of low-SNR astronomical images, and object detection benchmarking.

GWPAW 2025 Poster APS Global Physics Summit 2026 — Oral

OmniFormer: Context-aware transformer localizing transient noise sources in LIGO

Nov 2024 – Mar 2026 · Supervisor: Prof. Marco Cavaglià, Missouri University of Science and Technology

A transformer model that runs in inference mode to flag the likely source of transient, non-astronomical noise events inside advanced LIGO interferometers in real time.

Benchmarking Deep Learning Object Detection Models on a Feature-Deficient Dataset

Sep 2024 – May 2025

Evaluates common object detection models on a proprietary, low-SNR smartphone astrophotography dataset of over 5,000 labeled night-sky images across eight celestial objects and five constellations.
